# Best Home Inventory Apps in 2026: An Honest Comparison

We make one of the apps in this comparison.

That app is [StashDog](https://stashdog.io/download/). You should know that before reading anything else, because pretending to be a neutral third party would be weird and not especially convincing.

Home inventory apps are not all trying to solve the same problem. Some are built for families who want to find the spare charger without starting a household investigation. Some are built for small businesses managing supplies, tools, stock, and purchase orders. Some are closer to full home management systems. Some are basically a spreadsheet with better photos. And, honestly, a spreadsheet is still a perfectly valid choice for the right person.

The best home inventory app depends on what you are actually trying to do:

- Document belongings for insurance
- Make boxes, bins, closets, and storage units searchable
- Coordinate a shared household
- Track a collection
- Manage home-based business inventory
- Stop buying duplicate stuff because nobody knows where the first one went

## What to Look for in a Home Inventory App

Before comparing apps, get clear on the buying criteria. A home inventory app is only useful if you will actually keep using it after the first burst of motivation wears off.

### 1. Fast Capture

The app should let you add items quickly with photos, names, notes, values, tags, and locations. If every item feels like filing taxes, you will quit around item nine.

For most households, photo-first capture matters. You should be able to document what something looks like, where it lives, and any proof of ownership or value without building a museum catalog.

### 2. Search That Matches Real Life

You should be able to search the way humans remember things:

- "winter gloves"
- "extension cord"
- "passport"
- "holiday lights"
- "blue tote"
- "kids soccer gear"

The best home inventory software turns your house into something closer to a searchable database. That does not mean your home has to be perfectly organized. It means the information about your home has to be findable.

### 3. Locations, Containers, and QR Codes

Room-level inventory is helpful. Container-level inventory is better.

Homes are full of nested locations: garage shelves, closet bins, under-bed boxes, storage units, moving boxes, drawers, tool bags, cabinets, and random containers with names like "misc" because everyone eventually gives up.

QR codes help when physical storage gets messy. Scan a box and see what is inside. Search for an item and see which box contains it. That is the entire promise behind [Search-First Storage](https://stashdog.io/blog/search-first-storage-qr-code-box-organization/): you do not need perfect placement if retrieval works.

### 4. Sharing and Household Coordination

If more than one person moves things, the inventory has to support more than one person.

Family sharing, shared access, or multi-user support matters for:

- Parents tracking seasonal gear, school supplies, toys, tools, and sports equipment
- Roommates managing shared belongings
- Couples merging households
- Adult children helping parents document a home
- Small home-based businesses where someone else helps with inventory

An app that only works for one perfectly disciplined person is not really a household system. It is a personal shrine to optimism.

### 5. Insurance Documentation

For insurance use, look for:

- Photos
- Purchase dates
- Receipts or proof of purchase
- Serial numbers
- Model numbers
- Estimated values
- Exportable reports
- Cloud backup

A home inventory does not guarantee a claim result. Your policy, coverage, exclusions, deductibles, and insurer review still matter. But a structured record is a much better starting point than trying to remember every item in a room after something has gone wrong.

For a deeper insurance workflow, see [Home Inventory for Insurance](https://stashdog.io/blog/home-inventory-for-insurance/).

### 6. Pricing That Fits the Use Case

Business inventory pricing can make sense for businesses. It can feel ridiculous for households.

If an app starts from user licenses, inventory limits, purchase orders, role permissions, and commercial reporting, it may still be excellent software. It may also be more tool than your household needs.

The best price is not always the lowest price. The best price is the one attached to a workflow you will maintain.

## 2. Sortly

[Sortly](https://www.sortly.com/) is a serious inventory platform. It is also very clearly positioned around business inventory.

Its homepage describes Sortly as inventory software for small businesses managing supplies, materials, tools, equipment, and physical inventory. Its pricing page is structured around unique items, user licenses, QR and barcode labels, role permissions, purchase orders, QuickBooks integration, API/webhooks, and customer success.

That is not a criticism. It is a fit signal.

If you run a business, Sortly deserves a real look. If you are trying to manage stock, equipment, tools, consumables, purchase orders, team members, and commercial reporting, a business-first product may be exactly what you want.

### Sortly Pricing Snapshot

On the public pricing page checked for this draft, Sortly listed:

- Free plan: $0/month, 100 unique items, 1 user license
- Advanced: $24/month billed yearly during a first-year promotional price, with 500 unique items and 2 user licenses
- Ultra: $74/month billed yearly during a first-year promotional price, with 2,000 unique items and 5 user licenses
- Premium: $149/month billed yearly during a first-year promotional price, with 5,000 unique items and 8 user licenses
- Enterprise: quote-based

Verify this before publishing. Pricing pages change, discounts expire, and plan limits can move.

### Where Sortly Wins

Sortly is likely the better choice when:

- You are tracking business inventory
- Multiple employees need access
- You need barcode and QR label workflows
- You care about purchase orders, reporting, and inventory operations
- You want a mature commercial product with business-oriented controls

### Where Sortly Is Weaker for Homes

For ordinary home inventory, Sortly can be more than you need.

A 100-item free limit is enough to test the workflow, but a real household can blow past 100 items quickly once you include storage bins, electronics, tools, documents, decor, sports gear, kitchen overflow, kids' supplies, and seasonal items.

The bigger issue is not only price. It is posture. Sortly is designed around inventory management. StashDog is designed around household memory.

Those are related, but they are not the same.

## 3. DIY Spreadsheet

A spreadsheet is the most underrated competitor in this category.

Google Sheets, Excel, Airtable, Notion, Apple Numbers, or even a CSV file can absolutely work for home inventory if you are disciplined.

A simple home inventory spreadsheet might include:

- Item name
- Category
- Room
- Box or container
- Purchase date
- Estimated value
- Serial number
- Receipt link
- Photo link
- Notes

That is enough for many people. It is cheap, portable, easy to export, and not dependent on a specialized app.

### Where a Spreadsheet Wins

Choose a spreadsheet if:

- You want the cheapest possible system
- You like rows and columns
- You already live in Google Sheets or Excel
- You do not need QR codes or mobile-first capture
- You want full control over fields and formatting
- You are willing to manually manage photos and links

### Where a Spreadsheet Breaks Down

Spreadsheets are flexible because they are generic. That is also the problem.

They do not naturally model rooms, nested containers, shared household workflows, QR-coded boxes, photo capture, mobile item creation, or search-first retrieval. You can force all of that into a spreadsheet, but the maintenance burden grows fast.

The best spreadsheet users are organized enough that they may not need a home inventory app as badly in the first place. The rest of us need fewer blank cells and more "take photo, save item, find it later."

## 4. Itemtopia

[Itemtopia](https://www.itemtopia.com/) is one of the more established personal inventory apps and has a broad feature set for people who want detail.

Public materials describe support for customizable fields, receipt and expense features, warranty tracking, high-resolution photos, barcode scanning, offline access, categories, and multiple languages. That makes it a legitimate contender for collectors, careful catalogers, and users who want a flexible personal database.

### Where Itemtopia Wins

Itemtopia looks strongest when:

- You want detailed item records
- You like custom fields
- You track warranties, receipts, and expenses
- You care about collections or detailed personal databases
- You want a mature app with a lot of inventory-specific structure

### Where Itemtopia May Be Less Ideal

For overwhelmed users, a highly customizable database can feel like work. That is not Itemtopia's fault. It is the tradeoff of power.

If your main problem is messy boxes and household retrieval, the question is not "which app has the most fields?" The question is "which app will I actually use when I am tired and standing in the garage?"

If you need to make a storage unit searchable, document moving boxes, or stop rebuying lost household items, a narrower inventory app may feel faster. HomeZada is worth considering when inventory is part of a bigger homeowner management workflow.

Encircle makes sense when the job is professional documentation:

## How to Choose

Ask yourself five questions:

1. **Am I managing a home or a business?** If business, start with Sortly. If home, start with StashDog, Everspruce, Itemtopia, or a spreadsheet.
2. **Do I need to find things in boxes and storage locations?** If yes, prioritize search, container tracking, and QR workflows.
3. **Will multiple people use this?** If yes, check family sharing, multi-user support, and permissions.
4. **Do I need insurance documentation?** If yes, look for photos, receipts, serial numbers, values, exports, and cloud backup.
5. **Will I maintain this after the setup mood fades?** This is the real question. The best app is the one with the lowest realistic maintenance burden for your life.

Most people do not fail at home inventory because they chose the wrong custom field. They fail because the system asks for too much effort before it gives value back.

That is why we built StashDog around incremental capture. One box. One room. One shelf. One storage unit. Make a piece of the mess searchable, then keep going.
